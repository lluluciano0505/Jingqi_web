17:Tfff,<p>Every November, cartographers and spatial data enthusiasts around the world take on the <a href="https://30daymapchallenge.com/">#30DayMapChallenge</a> — one map per day, each following a daily theme. This is my 2025 entry, made as part of the MUSA program at the University of Pennsylvania's Weitzman School of Design.</p>
<p>18 maps. 18 themes. Some took an hour, some took a whole evening. All of them were an excuse to explore a new dataset, try a new technique, or just make something that looked interesting on a screen at midnight.</p>
<p><a href="https://lluluciano0505.github.io/30DayMapChallenge/"><strong>View all my maps →</strong></a></p>
<hr>
<p>Here's a quick run through what I made:</p>
<p><strong>Day 1 — Points:</strong> Heritage trees in Amsterdam — mapping the city's protected and candidate trees as a quiet portrait of urban green memory.</p>
<p><strong>Day 2 — Lines:</strong> The Bagua City street pattern of Tekes, Xinjiang — a city built around the eight trigrams of the I Ching, one of the few places in the world designed as a perfect octagonal grid.</p>
<p><strong>Day 3 — Polygons:</strong> Philadelphia zoning — residential, multi-family, and commercial mixed-use districts, mapping the legal geography that shapes everyday life in the city.</p>
<p><strong>Day 4 — My Data:</strong> Tracing my global footprints — a personal map of the cities I've called home across China, the UK, the Netherlands, and the US.</p>
<p><strong>Day 5 — Earth:</strong> Iceland terrain and major rivers — a hillshade elevation model highlighting the island's dramatic volcanic landscape.</p>
<p><strong>Day 6 — Dimensions:</strong> Golden Gate elevation in 3D hexagons — density and elevation of the Golden Gate area rendered as a spiked hexagonal grid in R.</p>
<p><strong>Day 7 — Accessibility:</strong> Chicago road accessibility by foodbank coverage — mapping which streets fall within 500m of a foodbank, and which don't.</p>
<p><strong>Day 8 — Urban:</strong> Utrecht city sprawling — building age composition across the city, showing how Utrecht expanded outward decade by decade.</p>
<p><strong>Day 9 — Analog:</strong> Hydraulic structure of the Polder system in the Jianghan Plain and Yangtze River Basin — a hand-drawn map of one of China's oldest water management systems.</p>
<p><strong>Day 10 — Air:</strong> Air traffic network from Cairo International Airport (CAI) — countries grouped by number of connected airports, visualizing CAI's global reach.</p>
<p><strong>Day 11 — Minimal:</strong> Russia railways — a network of steel arteries across eleven time zones, stripped down to its essentials.</p>
<p><strong>Day 12 — Map from 2125:</strong> Caspian Sea 2025→2125 — a speculative map of retreating shorelines based on current climate projections.</p>
<p><strong>Day 13 — 10-minute map:</strong> Africa population density — fast, simple, and surprisingly revealing about where people actually live on the continent.</p>
<p><strong>Day 14 — OpenStreetMap:</strong> Seoul convenience stores by brand (OSM) — mapping the hyper-dense convenience store landscape of Seoul, colored by chain.</p>
<p><strong>Day 15 — Fire:</strong> Southeast Asia recently active volcanoes — locating and classifying volcanic activity across the ring of fire.</p>
<p><strong>Day 16 — Cell:</strong> Beijing — a student's 2008 activity space — reconstructing movement patterns from memory and historical data.</p>
<p><strong>Day 17 — A new tool:</strong> Confessional patchwork of early modern Central Europe — experimenting with a new visualization tool to map religious geography after the Reformation.</p>
<p><strong>Day 18 — Out of this world:</strong> New Columbria — an imaginary new world, cartographically complete with mountain ranges, river systems, and coastlines that never existed.</p>
