<p>Raw coordinates are converted into structured visit events, classifying each stop as home or non-home. From there, home-based trips are reconstructed to ask two questions: <strong>How does the probability of returning home evolve as a trip unfolds?</strong> And do distinct mobility "types" emerge across users?</p>
<p>Hazard and survival models capture the return-home dynamics — showing that the pull toward home shifts predictably over the course of a day. User-level clustering then groups people by their mobility signatures, revealing that routine and exploration are not uniformly distributed across a population.</p>
