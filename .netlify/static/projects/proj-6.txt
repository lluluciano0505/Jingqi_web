17:T777,<p>Satellite imagery sees what ground surveys can't — but turning pixels into meaning requires the right models. This portfolio applies computer vision and deep learning to two distinct Earth observation problems, using Sentinel-2 multispectral data as the shared foundation.</p>
<p>{% include image.html url="<a href="https://lluluciano0505.github.io/vision-EO/">https://lluluciano0505.github.io/vision-EO/</a>" image="projects/proj-6/thumbnail.png" %}</p>
<p><strong>Part 1 — EuroSAT Land Cover Classification</strong></p>
<p>A CNN is trained to classify multispectral satellite tiles into 10 land-use categories — from industrial zones to forests, rivers, and croplands — using the EuroSAT benchmark derived from Sentinel-2 imagery. Traditional ML baselines establish the performance floor; the CNN shows what spatial feature learning adds on top.</p>
<p><strong>Part 2 — 2025 LA Palisades Fire Damage Prediction</strong></p>
<p>In January 2025, the Palisades fire devastated residential areas across Los Angeles. Using pre- and post-fire Sentinel-2 imagery, a <strong>U-Net semantic segmentation model</strong> predicts structural damage at the parcel level. The model integrates Digital Elevation Model (DEM) aspect data — encoding how terrain shape drives fire spread — and is validated against CAL FIRE DINS field assessments on the ground.</p>
<p>The result is a topographically-aware damage prediction pipeline that operationalizes satellite imagery for rapid post-disaster assessment, with implications for insurance triage, resource deployment, and long-term rebuilding planning.</p>
