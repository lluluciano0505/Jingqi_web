1a:T598,<p>False alarms make up 77% of all fire-related incidents in Philadelphia, pulling resources from real emergencies. This project builds a predictive model to flag high-probability false alarms before dispatch.</p>
<p>Six datasets were integrated — NFIRS incident records, Census ACS demographics, L&#x26;I fire-alarm compliance data for 26,310 buildings, hourly weather from PHL airport, fire station locations, and urban geography — into three logistic regression models of increasing complexity. The final model achieves AUC 0.672, identifying weather conditions, educational attainment, and building compliance as the strongest predictors.</p>
<p>The model also surfaces equity concerns: some demographic patterns persist in false-alarm rates even after controlling for environment and operations, demanding careful policy interpretation.</p>
<p>A 0.4 probability threshold is recommended for deployment — enabling lighter-response packages on high-confidence false alarms, paired with targeted community education and enforcement.</p>
