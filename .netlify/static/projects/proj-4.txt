17:T62c,<p>Cities pulse in patterns. This project asks whether a neural network can learn them well enough to predict where people will move next.</p>
<p>Using <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01754-0">YJMob100K</a> — anonymized mobile phone trajectories discretized into 500m × 500m grid cells at 30-minute intervals — an LSTM model predicts the next inflow or outflow for each cell, given 4 hours of prior movement (8 timesteps). Feature vectors combine current flow values, ~85 POI-category dimensions queried via DuckDB, and sinusoidal time encodings.</p>
<p>{% include image.html url="<a href="https://lluluciano0505.github.io/AI_Mobility/ai_mobility_flow_prediction.html">https://lluluciano0505.github.io/AI_Mobility/ai_mobility_flow_prediction.html</a>" image="projects/proj-4/thumbnail.png" %}</p>
<p>A linear baseline serves as the comparison. It fails catastrophically — performing <strong>more than two orders of magnitude worse</strong> than the LSTM (test MSE ≈ 4.15 vs. baseline's collapse). Temporal sequence isn't optional; ignoring it destroys predictive power.</p>
<p>The LSTM accurately reproduces citywide flow structure across space and time. Remaining errors concentrate in high-intensity, high-volatility zones — pointing toward future work with attention mechanisms or spatial graph neural networks.</p>
